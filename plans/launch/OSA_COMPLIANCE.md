## Why we're in scope (and only lightly)

The Online Safety Act 2023 regulates **user-to-user (U2U) services** — any service where content
generated by one user may be encountered by another. Concertable has **artist↔venue messaging**, so it
is technically a U2U service with links to the UK and is **in scope**. Ofcom is the regulator; the
illegal-content duties are in force.

But scope ≠ heavy obligations. The Act is **proportionate to the service's risk and size**, and
Concertable sits at the low-risk floor:

- **Closed, identity-verified accounts.** Users are venues/artists onboarded through Stripe Connect KYC
  and DAC7 identity collection. No anonymous accounts, no open signup-and-post.
- **Private 1:1 messaging only.** Messaging is between two parties already transacting a booking. There
  is **no public posting, no feed, no broadcast, no comments, no user profiles open to the world**.
- **Business context, not social.** The purpose is negotiating and settling paid gigs, not open
  social interaction.
- **Not aimed at or likely accessed by children** (see the children's-access assessment below).

This is the same duty every UK booking platform with messaging carries (GigPig, GigXchange included) —
it is real launch work, but for our shape it is **"document the low-risk assessment + provide a
reporting/complaints route,"** not content-moderation infrastructure.

> **Scope note:** this pack covers the **B2B platform only.** The customer-facing marketplace (public
> event listings + reviews) is materially more in-scope and is deferred with its own OSA work in
> [`../marketplace/MARKETPLACE_PLAN.md`](../marketplace/MARKETPLACE_PLAN.md). Do **not** treat this pack
> as covering that.

## Artifact 1 — Illegal-content risk assessment

*Ofcom requires an in-scope U2U service to assess the risk of users encountering illegal content and of
the service being used to commit/facilitate priority offences, then take proportionate mitigations.*

### Service description

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Service | Concertable B2B — venue↔artist booking + settlement SaaS |
| U2U functionality | **Private 1:1 messaging** between a venue tenant and an artist tenant, scoped to a booking/opportunity context |
| Other user-generated content | Org profile fields (name, bio, images), opportunity/gig listings — visible to other authenticated business users, not the public web |
| User base | UK venues and artists, onboarded via Stripe Connect KYC + DAC7 identity. No anonymous or self-serve-public accounts |
| Reach of any one message | Exactly one counterparty. No broadcast, feed, group-public posting, or virality mechanic |
| Discovery / recommender | None over user messages. Search is over opportunities/orgs, not message content |
| Encryption | Messages stored server-side, not end-to-end encrypted (we can action reports) |

### Risk rating against Ofcom's priority illegal-content categories

Rated **for our specific functionality** (private 1:1 messaging between KYC'd businesses). Rationale is
the same across most rows: no anonymity, no public reach, no minors, transactional context, full
server-side visibility for takedown.

| Priority category | Risk | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Terrorism | **Low** | No public reach or broadcast; closed business accounts |
| CSEA (child sexual exploitation/abuse) | **Low** | No child users; no image-sharing feed; 1:1 KYC'd business channel |
| Hate / harassment / stalking / threats | **Low–Med** | The one plausible vector — a dispute between counterparties could turn abusive. Mitigated by report route + account identity + ability to remove/suspend |
| Fraud & financial offences | **Low–Med** | Off-platform-payment / bait-and-switch attempts possible. Mitigated by on-platform settlement being the product's core, identity KYC, and reporting |
| Drugs / firearms / weapons supply | **Low** | No marketplace-for-goods surface; music-booking context |
| Controlling/coercive behaviour, intimate-image abuse, extreme porn, suicide/self-harm content | **Low** | No image feed, no anonymity, no minors, business context |
| Proceeds of crime / money laundering | **Low** | Stripe Connect + KYC/DAC7 sit on the money path; platform-agent posture |

### Overall assessment

**Low risk.** The genuine (still low–medium) vectors are (a) abusive messages in a soured booking
dispute and (b) attempts to move a deal off-platform to defraud a counterparty. Both are adequately
mitigated by the measures below — no proactive content scanning or moderation team is proportionate at
this scale.

### Proportionate mitigations (what we actually do)

- [ ] In-app **report route** on every message thread + a monitored inbox (Artifact 2).
- [ ] **Complaints/appeals** process (Artifact 4).
- [ ] **Terms of service** prohibiting illegal content and setting out enforcement — **[LEGAL]** (belongs
  in the platform T&Cs the solicitor is already drafting; cross-reference, don't duplicate).
- [ ] **Account identity** (Stripe KYC / DAC7) — already shipped; a real-world identity behind every
  account is itself a strong deterrent.
- [ ] Ability to **remove a message / suspend a tenant** on a substantiated report — **[DECIDE]** confirm
  the admin capability exists or is built (see "Code slice" below).
- [ ] **Review date:** re-assess on any material functionality change (e.g. group messaging, public
  reviews, or marketplace switch-on) and at least annually.

## Artifact 2 — Illegal-content reporting route

*Requirement: an easy, always-available way for any user to report illegal content, and a process for us
to act on it.*

### In-app (the code slice)

- A **"Report"** action on each message (and/or each thread) in both manager SPAs.
- Opens a short form: reason (dropdown: illegal content / harassment / fraud / spam / other) + free-text.
- Submitting emails a structured report to the safety inbox with: reporter tenant, reported tenant,
  thread/message id, category, timestamp, and message excerpt.
- Confirms to the reporter that it was received and will be reviewed.

### Fallback / always-available

- A published **`report@` (or `safety@`) email** on the T&Cs / cookies / footer legal pages, so a report
  is possible even without the in-app button. **[DECIDE]** which address — likely route to the same
  monitored inbox as `support@`.

### Handling

1. Report lands in the monitored safety inbox.
2. Triage against the takedown SLA (Artifact 3).
3. Act: no action / warn / remove content / suspend account, recorded (Artifact 4 keeps the log).
4. Notify reporter and (where appropriate) the reported party of the outcome + appeal route.

## Artifact 3 — Illegal-content takedown SLA (internal)

*Requirement: act on illegal content swiftly. These are internal targets, sized for a pre-launch
low-risk B2B service; tighten as volume grows.*

| Stage | Target |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge report to reporter | Automated on submit (immediate) |
| Triage / first human review | **1 working day** |
| Remove content assessed as illegal | **Promptly on assessment — same working day** where clear-cut |
| Complex / ambiguous cases (legal judgement needed) | Escalate to solicitor; interim measure (hide pending review) within 1 working day |
| Reporter + reported party notified of outcome | Within **2 working days** of resolution |

- **[DECIDE]** Who is on the rota at launch (you). Document the escalation contact (solicitor) for
  ambiguous illegality.
- Keep an internal record of each report and action (feeds Artifact 4 and any Ofcom information request).

## Artifact 4 — Complaints & appeals process

*Requirement: a mechanism for users to complain (about content, about a moderation decision, or about
our compliance with duties) and to appeal an action taken against them.*

### Grounds a user can raise

- Content they reported was not actioned.
- Content **they posted** was removed, or their account was actioned, and they dispute it.
- They believe the service is not meeting its safety duties.

### Process

1. Complaint via the same **`report@`/`safety@`** route (label it a complaint/appeal), or a reply to the
   outcome notification.
2. Logged with timestamp, complainant, subject, and the original decision reference.
3. **Reviewed by someone other than the original decision-maker where practicable** — at launch, with a
   solo operator, this means a documented fresh review; note the constraint honestly.
4. Outcome communicated within **5 working days**, with reasons.
5. Records retained (see retention policy — align with the privacy policy the solicitor drafts). **[LEGAL]**

### Children's-access assessment [LEGAL]

The Act requires assessing whether the service is **likely to be accessed by children**. Draft position:
**not likely** — Concertable is a B2B service; accounts are businesses onboarded through Stripe KYC/DAC7,
the content and purpose are commercial gig booking, and there is no offering aimed at or attractive to
children. If the solicitor agrees it is not likely to be accessed by children, the **children's-risk
duties do not apply** and only the illegal-content duties above remain. Document the conclusion either
way — the *assessment* is required even when the answer is "no."
